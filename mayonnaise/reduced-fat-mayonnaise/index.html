Real mayonnaise, with a 70-80% oil content, is thick, spoonable, creamy, opaque and doesn't flow. Such a high oil concentration is, of course, high in fat. So in recent years, the search has been on to produce mayonnaise with all the taste and textural qualities which people love - but with less calories. Try to reduce the oil content of your mayonnaise, however, in pursuit of a lower fat product and you can end up with something which flows like single cream.

Reduced fat mayonnaise, which meets consumer demands for healthier product choices, has to be able to mimic the above properties of real mayonnaise. We can show you how. As well as preventing separation between the oil and water, these large hydrocolloid molecules are able to mix with the oil globules and inhibit movement - meaning your mayonnaise stays thick, creamy and opaque.

How we can help

Three ways into this product.

Whether you are building it new, fixing something that goes wrong in production, or looking for cost in the recipe.

New product

We can help you develop a range of reduced fat mayonnaise products. Development depends on the end product use. These include:

  • Mixing with raw, fresh vegetables
  • Use in a layered salad or coleslaw
  • Mixed with a cooked product such as pasta salad, egg mayonnaise or coronation chicken
  • Mixed with a dairy product such as fromage frais or yogurt as in dips
  • Use in intermediate products such as sandwiches - which requires a good shelf life in the bucket but only two days when applied to the sandwich

All will require stabilisation during the aqueous phase - but the mouthfeel characteristics and texture will be very different.

KaTech can advise you on all aspects of new product development including:

  • Types of stabiliser available and their properties in the end product
  • Optimal recipe formulations dependant on the oil content brief
  • Clean label alternative formulations and their use
  • Production process
  • Follow through until end of shelf life

Contact us today for advice.

Troubleshooting

Our approach to problem solving is based firstly on ensuring you have the right mayonnaise for your chosen end use. A reduced fat mayonnaise which may work perfectly in a sandwich may be completely unsuitable for use in a layered salad, for example.

We can also help you find solutions to some of the adverse reactions which can occur when producing a lower fat mayonnaise.

These include:

  • Splitting (due to water starvation of the emulsion) when some clean label starches are used - we can help you establish different production techniques to avoid this
  • Calcium reaction when some fibres are mixed with other ingredients such as tomato paste or onion, leading to slow gelation or lump formation
  • Too much starch which can lead to undesirably sticky or claggy textures
  • Methods of incorporating the starch when there is not enough oil to form a slurry

Our team has extensive experience of these potential adverse reactions during development - and how to avoid them. We can help you solve the problem or find a work-around solution. We can also advise on which materials to avoid in certain developments and which combinations will help you achieve the required mouthfeel and textures.

Contact us today for advice on tackling your product issues.

Cost optimisation

The basic ingredients of mayonnaise are oil, water, egg and vinegar. Because oil is a relatively expensive ingredient, costs can be reduced when lower oil formulations are put together. The balancing factor, however, is the cost of the water mobility control agents.

At KaTech we can advise on:

  • The best balance between oil content and quality performance
  • The optimisation of ingredients and the risk of lowering beyond certain limits
  • Egg options (including free range or standard)

Talk to our team today about how we can help you produce a better reduced fat mayonnaise - for less.
